RE: DOL-OESE-33831 — Innovative Approaches to Literacy 84.215G Office of Elementary and Secondary Education Posted at: https://www.grants.gov/search-results-detail/361808 Dear Office of Elementary and Secondary Education Grants Program Officer, New Roots Tutoring Collective is a Columbus, Ohio nonprofit founded in 2019 that provides free after-school tutoring, English-language support, and mentoring to refugee and immigrant youth in grades 3–12. We serve students from Bhutanese, Somali, Congolese, and Afghan families, most of whom arrived in central Ohio within the last five years and are the first in their families to navigate U.S. public schools. This letter confirms our intent to apply for the Innovative Approaches to Literacy 84.215G opportunity. Our work is built on the belief that a child’s first years in a new country should be a beginning, not a barrier. In the 2024–2025 school year, with community grant support, we operated three neighborhood tutoring sites hosted in partnership with Columbus City Schools and the Ethiopian Tewahedo Social Services resettlement agency. We served 142 students across our Northland, Hilltop, and Whitehall sites, delivering over 6,800 hours of one-on-one and small-group tutoring. We matched 61 trained volunteer tutors with students, supported by four part-time bilingual program coordinators. By year’s end, 88% of our students were promoted to the next grade, and we helped 19 high school seniors complete college or trade-school applications. These outcomes reflect the direct connection between consistent, language-rich literacy support and academic persistence for newcomer youth. Our families consistently tell us the after-school hours are when learning gaps either close or widen. Demand has outgrown our volunteer model: waitlists at all three sites now exceed 30 students. Our coordinators—the people who keep tutors trained and families engaged—are funded entirely on soft money, and we cannot sustain them past the current year without new operating support. The Innovative Approaches to Literacy program aligns directly with our mission to expand equitable access to high-quality literacy instruction for underserved students. We intend to use this funding to add a fourth site in the Morse Road corridor and convert two coordinator roles from part-time to full-time, so we can serve an additional 60 students and stop turning families away. Our greatest need is flexible operating funding for staff salaries and learning supplies—the unglamorous core that makes everything else possible. Submission deadline: 06/09/2026 Respectfully, Amara Okonkwo Executive Director New Roots Tutoring Collective
